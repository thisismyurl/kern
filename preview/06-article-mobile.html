The Last Ferry to Pelee

Every August the Vosloo family closes the cottage the same weekend, and every August someone insists on taking the last boat instead of the first.

Bram Vosloo Sep 2, 2026
The Pelee Islander II, dusk crossing

The dock at Leamington smelled the way it always did in the last week of August: diesel and whitefish and the particular sweetness of a season ending on schedule. Owen had made this crossing eleven times, always on the nine o'clock, always with his mother's cooler wedged between his knees. This year the cooler was empty and his mother was three weeks gone.

"You'll want to stand on the port side," she said, not looking at him. "The sun does something to the water past the lighthouse."

Nobody on this deck was waiting for him to be someone other than exactly who he was standing there being.

They didn't speak again until the lighthouse. When it came, the water did exactly what she said it would: it turned the colour of something you'd want to bottle and couldn't.

"She'd have liked this crossing," he said finally, to no one in particular.

"They usually do," she said. "The ones who wait for the last boat instead of the first."
